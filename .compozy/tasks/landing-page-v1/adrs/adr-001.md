# ADR-001: Classic Brazilian Infoproduct Page Structure

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-19

## Context

The ORGANIZE-$E workshop needs a sales landing page that converts cold traffic into Kiwify purchases (R$ 47). The target audience is Brazilian women who struggle with financial disorganization. The page must be mobile-first, use a countdown timer for urgency (event date: June 28, 2026), and follow design conventions familiar to Brazilian digital product buyers (Hotmart/Kiwify ecosystem). Three structural approaches were evaluated.

## Decision

Use the Classic Brazilian Infoproduct page structure — an 8-section layout proven in the Brazilian infoproduct market:

1. Hero (responsive images, no text overlay, countdown timer)
2. Pain qualifier — "Para quem é este workshop"
3. Testimonials cluster (placed after pain qualification, before price)
4. Workshop deliverables — "O que você vai encontrar"
5. Bio — "Quem vai te guiar" (Rafaela Ribeiro)
6. Price block with countdown timer + CTA
7. 7-day guarantee seal (bright, prominent)
8. FAQ + Final CTA + Post-event waitlist fallback

Multiple CTA buttons (top, middle, bottom) all pointing to `https://pay.kiwify.com.br/3qkvzij`. Women-first copy throughout.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative B: Minimalist Premium

- **Description**: Shorter page with single CTA block, betting on design clarity and Rafaela's authority
- **Pros**: Faster load, modern aesthetic, easier to build
- **Cons**: Fewer conversion touchpoints; less social proof exposure; lower ceiling for cold traffic
- **Why rejected**: For a new creator with limited existing audience, trust-building signals (testimonials, guarantee, FAQ) are essential — cutting them risks conversion loss

### Alternative C: Story-First Emotional Journey

- **Description**: Leads with Rafaela's personal narrative before revealing the workshop offer
- **Pros**: Highly relatable for female audience; strong brand differentiation
- **Cons**: CTA buried deep; impulsive buyers may drop off before seeing the price
- **Why rejected**: Immediate purchase is the primary goal; delaying the offer too long conflicts with the conversion objective

## Consequences

### Positive

- Follows structure Brazilian digital product buyers are conditioned to expect
- Multiple CTAs reduce drop-off by giving buyers multiple entry points to checkout
- Testimonial placement (after pain qualification) catches visitors at peak emotional readiness
- FAQ section preemptively removes purchase objections

### Negative

- Longer page requires careful mobile layout and image optimization to prevent slow loads
- More copy sections means more content to maintain if Rafaela updates the offer

### Risks

- Page length on mobile may feel long — mitigated by scroll-triggered animations and sticky CTA visibility
- Countdown timer becomes irrelevant after June 28 — mitigated by post-event waitlist fallback logic

## Implementation Notes

- Post-event state: hide the countdown timer and Kiwify CTA; show a waitlist email capture form instead
- Guarantee seal must be brightly colored (contrasting with the black/white palette) and placed above the final CTA
- Hero section uses responsive images (`hero1.png`, `hero2.png`, `hero3.png`) with no text overlay per requirements
